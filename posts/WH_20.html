Stress and a late period: considering other causes

Stress can accompany cycle changes, but pregnancy, weight changes, heavy exercise, medicines and illness can also delay periods.

PV
Pulse & Vitality • Health education
Primary reference: NHS — Missed or late periods ↗ • Updated: 2026-10-26 • Clinical review: Not recorded
💡 Key Actionable Takeaway

Test for pregnancy when relevant. Note changes in routine, food intake and training. Support sleep, regular meals and manageable stress relief, and follow up if bleeding does not return.

Understanding the topic

Stress can accompany cycle changes, but pregnancy, weight changes, heavy exercise, medicines and illness can also delay periods. One unusual month does not establish excessive cortisol as the cause. 1 2 3

What you can do

Test for pregnancy when relevant. Note changes in routine, food intake and training. Support sleep, regular meals and manageable stress relief, and follow up if bleeding does not return.

What the evidence cannot tell you

Pulse and HRV do not measure cortisol or distinguish causes of amenorrhoea. Self-prescribed cortisol products can delay appropriate assessment.

When to seek advice

Seek care for three missed periods, persistent cycle changes or accompanying symptoms such as substantial weight change.

Sources and what they support

  3. Mountjoy et al. (2023) — IOC consensus statement on Relative Energy Deficiency in Sport (REDs) — Consensus statement. Supports: Low energy availability, bone, menstrual health and multidisciplinary assessment.
