Demystifying Day Strain (0–21): The Logarithmic Scale of Daily Load

How cardiovascular time-in-zone and resistance muscular load are compiled into a non-linear score from 0 to 21.

PV
Pulse & Vitality Clinical Editorial Board
Clinical Wearable Reference: WHOOP Science • Mapped Screen: Activity Screen • Metric: Day Strain (0–21)
💡 Key Actionable Takeaway

Strain uses a logarithmic scale; pushing from 14 to 18 requires exponential cardiovascular effort compared to moving from 0 to 10.

1. What is Day Strain?

Day Strain is an aggregate metric from 0.0 to 21.0 that quantifies the total physiological load imposed on your body over a 24-hour cycle. It does not simply track athletic workouts; it accounts for the cumulative cardiac and muscular cost of your entire day—from your morning run to pacing during a presentation or lifting groceries.

2. The Logarithmic Nature of the Scale

Strain is built upon a modified Borg physiological exertion curve, meaning it is non-linear and logarithmic:
- 0.0 – 9.9 (Light Load): Minimal stress on the body. Typical of a rest day or light desk work.
- 10.0 – 13.9 (Moderate Load): Solid aerobic maintenance. Meaningful cardiovascular stimulus without excessive recovery demands.
- 14.0 – 17.9 (High Load): Demanding workout, strenuous endurance session, or heavy strength block.
- 18.0 – 21.0 (All-Out Effort): Extreme physical expenditure, comparable to running an ultra-marathon or competing in a multi-stage athletic event.

Why Logarithmic? Moving from a 0 to a 10 requires a moderate amount of work, but moving from a 14 to an 18 requires exponentially more cardiovascular effort.

3. Integrating Muscular Load and Cardio Strain

Traditional fitness trackers only read cardiovascular strain via optical heart rate sensors. Advanced strain engines integrate accelerometer and gyroscopic data to capture Muscular Load during strength training. High-load resistance sets elevate intra-muscular pressure and mechanical fatigue, ensuring that heavy squat sessions are accurately credited even if heart rates drop between sets.
