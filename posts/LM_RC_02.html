Overnight Resting Heart Rate (RHR): Your Window into Myocardial Efficiency

How nocturnal resting pulse reflects stroke volume, myocardial efficiency, and acute systemic stressors like alcohol, dehydration, or viral infection.

PV
Pulse & Vitality Clinical Editorial Board
Clinical Wearable Reference: Fitbit Cardio Health / AHA • Mapped Screen: Recovery Screen • Metric: Resting Heart Rate (RHR)
💡 Key Actionable Takeaway

A sudden jump in overnight resting heart rate is an early indicator that your heart is working overtime to clear alcohol, counter dehydration, or fight an infection.

1. What is Nocturnal Resting Heart Rate?

Resting Heart Rate (RHR) measures the minimum number of times your heart contracts per minute while in a state of complete rest. The app measures your nocturnal baseline during your deepest, most stable slow-wave sleep periods to eliminate the confounding variables of movement, posture, and emotional arousal.

2. The Physiology of Stroke Volume

Your cardiac output ($Q$) is the product of Stroke Volume ($SV$) and Heart Rate ($HR$):
$$Q = SV \times HR$$
As aerobic fitness and myocardial hypertrophy improve, your left ventricle increases its chamber volume and contractile force, pumping more blood per contraction. Consequently, your resting heart rate drops to maintain steady systemic perfusion. Elite endurance athletes often exhibit resting heart rates between 38 and 48 bpm.

3. Acute Overnight Spikes: What Do They Mean?

While long-term declines in RHR indicate cardiovascular conditioning, an acute overnight spike (+4 to +8 bpm above baseline) serves as an immediate physiological red flag:
- Alcohol Clearance: Hepatic clearance of ethanol and acetaldehyde demands vasodilation and increased cardiac output throughout the sleep cycle.
- Dehydration: Reduced plasma blood volume forces the heart to beat faster to supply peripheral tissues.
- Immune Mobilization: Pyrogenic cytokines released during viral incubation elevate core temperature and heart rate hours before physical symptoms manifest.
- Late Digestion: Gastrointestinal perfusion prevents nocturnal cardiac down-regulation.
