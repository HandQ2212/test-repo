Clinical Evidence for Male Pelvic Training: BJU International and PubMed Landmark Studies

The peer-reviewed clinical research proving pelvic floor rehabilitation resolves erectile dysfunction and lifelong premature ejaculation.

💡 Key Actionable Takeaway

Peer-reviewed clinical trials in BJU International and PubMed demonstrate that targeted pelvic floor training reverses erectile dysfunction and premature ejaculation in up to 75–82% of patients.

1. Is There Scientific Evidence for Male Kegels?

Yes. While pelvic floor muscle training (PFMT) was originally popularized for women by Dr. Arnold Kegel in 1948, decades of modern urological clinical research have established that targeted pelvic floor physical therapy is a first-line clinical intervention for male sexual dysfunction.

2. Landmark Study 1: Resolving Erectile Dysfunction (BJU International, 2005)

In a landmark randomized controlled trial conducted at Somerset Nuffield Hospital (Dorey et al., published in BJU International, PMID: 16011707):
- 55 men aged 20–78 with chronic erectile dysfunction (ED) of at least 6 months' duration were assigned to an intensive 6-month pelvic floor muscle training program.
- Results: 40.0% of men regained completely normal erectile function, and an additional 35.5% demonstrated significant clinical improvement, yielding an overall 75.5% success rate.
- Conclusion: Targeted pelvic floor exercises should be prescribed as a first-line non-pharmacological treatment for erectile dysfunction prior to pharmaceutical interventions (PDE-5 inhibitors).

3. Landmark Study 2: Treating Lifelong Premature Ejaculation (PubMed, 2014)

In a prospective clinical trial published in Therapeutic Advances in Urology (Pastore et al., PMID: 24716983):
- Men suffering from lifelong premature ejaculation (intra-vaginal ejaculatory latency time under 60 seconds) underwent 12 weeks of pelvic floor muscle rehabilitation.
- Results: 82.5% of patients achieved voluntary control over their ejaculatory reflex, increasing their average latency time by more than fourfold (from 31.7 seconds to 146.4 seconds).

4. Landmark Study 3: The Role of Pelvic Stretching (Sexual Medicine Reviews, 2016)

Research published by Deborah S. Cohen et al. established that pelvic floor rehabilitation must pair strengthening with stretching (pelvic flexibility) to relieve chronic pudendal nerve entrapment and optimize blood perfusion.
