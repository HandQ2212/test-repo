Decoding Your Sleep Score: How the Algorithm Evaluates Rest

An inside look at the multi-parametric algorithm that evaluates your sleep duration, restorative slow-wave stages, and nocturnal heart rate dip.

PV
Pulse & Vitality Clinical Editorial Board
Clinical Wearable Reference: Fitbit Sleep Science / WHOOP • Mapped Screen: Sleep Screen • Metric: Sleep Score (0–100)
💡 Key Actionable Takeaway

A high Sleep Score requires both adequate duration and a distinct nocturnal heart rate dip during deep sleep stages.

1. What is the Sleep Score?

The Sleep Score is an aggregated biometric index ranging from 0 to 100 that quantifies the true restorative quality of your night. Rather than treating sleep as a passive block of time, the algorithm analyzes the physiological transitions between light sleep, deep Slow Wave Sleep (SWS), and Rapid Eye Movement (REM).

2. The Three Core Pillars of the Algorithm

  1. Sleep Duration & Sleep Need (40% Weight):
    The algorithm compares your actual Total Sleep Time (TST) against your individualized Sleep Need. Your baseline sleep need is calculated from rolling 30-day averages, recent day strain, accumulated sleep debt, and physical recovery demands.
  2. Restorative Sleep Architecture (35% Weight):
    Time spent in bed is meaningless if your sleep architecture is fragmented. The score heavily weights time spent in deep Slow Wave Sleep (cellular repair) and REM (neuro-cognitive restoration). An optimal night comprises at least 35%–45% combined restorative sleep.
  3. Nocturnal Cardiovascular Restoration (25% Weight):
    When you enter deep sleep, your parasympathetic nervous system should take full control, causing a pronounced drop in heart rate (the 'nocturnal dip') below your waking resting baseline. If elevated resting heart rate or high sleep stress persists, restoration points are deducted.

3. Interpreting Your Daily Score

  • 90–100 (Optimal / Excellent): Superior restorative sleep with an optimal nocturnal dip and full sleep-need satisfaction. Peak physical and cognitive readiness.
  • 80–89 (Good): Sufficient restoration for baseline physiological maintenance and moderate-to-high training loads.
  • 60–79 (Fair): Elevated sleep fragmentation or unfulfilled sleep debt. Expect reduced autonomic flexibility and reaction time.
  • Below 60 (Poor): Severe sleep debt or physiological distress. Prioritize active recovery and an early bedtime.
