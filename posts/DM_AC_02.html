Hiểu đúng

Mệt sau một buổi nặng khác với hiệu suất giảm kéo dài nhiều tuần. Hội chứng tập quá sức cần đánh giá chuyên môn và loại trừ bệnh, thiếu năng lượng, thiếu máu cùng các nguyên nhân khác; chưa có một chỉ số HRV hay xét nghiệm đơn lẻ xác định được. 1 2 3

Bạn có thể làm gì?

Giới hạn cần biết

Một đợt deload theo số ngày cố định không bảo đảm chữa được quá sức. HRV có thể tăng hoặc giảm trong nhiều tình trạng; không dùng riêng nó để xác định nguyên nhân.

Khi nào cần tư vấn?

Khám nếu mệt và giảm hiệu suất kéo dài, sụt cân ngoài ý muốn, mất kinh hoặc chấn thương tái diễn.

Training fatigue and persistent underperformance

Fatigue after a hard session differs from a sustained drop in performance.

PV
Pulse & Vitality • Health education
Primary reference: Meeusen et al. (2013) — Prevention, diagnosis, and treatment of the overtraining syndrome ↗ • Updated: 2026-10-26 • Clinical review: Not recorded
💡 Key Actionable Takeaway

When fatigue accumulates, reduce training stress and record sleep, food intake, mood and performance. Resume progressively as these improve. Use a flexible plan rather than chasing device targets.

Understanding the topic

Fatigue after a hard session differs from a sustained drop in performance. Overtraining syndrome requires professional evaluation and exclusion of illness, low energy availability, anaemia and other causes. No single HRV reading or laboratory marker establishes it. 1 2 3

What you can do

When fatigue accumulates, reduce training stress and record sleep, food intake, mood and performance. Resume progressively as these improve. Use a flexible plan rather than chasing device targets.

What the evidence cannot tell you

A fixed deload duration is not a guaranteed treatment. HRV can move in either direction for different reasons and cannot identify the cause alone.

When to seek advice

Seek assessment for persistent underperformance, unintended weight loss, missed periods or repeated injuries.

Sources and what they support

  1. Meeusen et al. (2013) — Prevention, diagnosis, and treatment of the overtraining syndrome — Joint consensus statement. Supports: Overtraining, exclusion diagnosis and absence of a single marker.

  2. Kellmann et al. (2018) — Recovery and Performance in Sport: Consensus Statement — Consensus statement. Supports: Individual recovery, training load and life demands.

  3. Mountjoy et al. (2023) — IOC consensus statement on Relative Energy Deficiency in Sport (REDs) — Consensus statement. Supports: Low energy availability, bone, menstrual health and multidisciplinary assessment.

Content and sources updated: 2026-10-26. Educational content; clinical sign-off has not been recorded.
