Nutrition and sperm health: evidence limits

Diet contributes to reproductive health, but semen measurements are not the same as pregnancy or live-birth outcomes.

PV
Pulse & Vitality • Health education
Primary reference: AUA/ASRM — Diagnosis and treatment of infertility in men: guideline part II ↗ • Mapped Screen: Reproductive Health & Sperm Analysis Screen • Biometric: Nutrition and sperm health: evidence limits (educational topic; not a measured biomarker)
💡 Key Actionable Takeaway

Prioritize varied food and discuss smoking, health, medicines and conception plans. Assess both partners when conception is difficult rather than relying on sperm supplements. Check overlapping product ingredients.

Understanding the topic

Diet contributes to reproductive health, but semen measurements are not the same as pregnancy or live-birth outcomes. Male-fertility guidance notes uncertain clinical benefit for many antioxidant supplements. 1 2 3

What you can do

Prioritize varied food and discuss smoking, health, medicines and conception plans. Assess both partners when conception is difficult rather than relying on sperm supplements. Check overlapping product ingredients.

What the evidence cannot tell you

Do not promise that a food repairs sperm DNA or raises pregnancy rates by a fixed amount. Dietary associations are not proof of treatment efficacy.

When to seek advice

Difficulty conceiving, pain or testicular changes warrant assessment rather than waiting through repeated supplement courses.

Sources and what they support

  1. AUA/ASRM — Diagnosis and treatment of infertility in men: guideline part II — Clinical practice guideline. Supports: Limited supplement evidence and testosterone-related fertility concerns.

  2. ASRM (2022) — Optimizing natural fertility: a committee opinion — Committee opinion. Supports: Intercourse timing, prediction limitations and fertility evaluation.

  3. WHO — Healthy diet — Public health guidance. Supports: Dietary variety and food quality.

Content and sources updated: 2026-10-26. Educational content; clinical sign-off has not been recorded.
