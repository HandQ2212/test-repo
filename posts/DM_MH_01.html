Inside the Spinal Ejaculatory Reflex: Emission vs. Expulsion

In medical physiology, male sexual response is famously summarized by the neurological mnemonic "Point and Shoot"—because erection and ejaculation are governed by opposing branches of your Autonomic Nervous System (Giuliano & Clement, 2005; Alwaal et al., 2015): - Erection ("Point") is Parasympathetic (S2–S4): Requires vagal calmness, low adrenaline, slow nasal breathing, and nitric oxide vasodilation. - Ejaculation ("Shoot") is Sympathetic & Somatic (T10–L2 & S2–S4): Triggered when sympathetic nervous system excitation climbs to a critical threshold, firing a two-phase spinal reflex: 1. Phase 1 — Emission (Sympathetic Thoracolumbar T10–L2): Sympathetic nerves contract the smooth muscle of the vas deferens, seminal vesicles, and prostate gland, squeezing seminal fluid into the posterior (prostatic) urethra while closing the bladder neck. The moment emission completes, a man reaches the clinical Point of No Return (PONR)—experienced subjectively as the sensation of "inevitability." 2. Phase 2 — Expulsion (Somatic Pudendal Nerve S2–S4): Sensory pressure inside the prostatic urethra triggers the spinal ejaculatory generator (lumbar spinothalamic / LSt cells), which commands the striated Bulbocavernosus (BC) and Pubococcygeus (PC) muscles to fire involuntary, rhythmic contractions every 0.8 seconds.

Why do men experience rapid or premature ejaculation? Because high sympathetic tone (anxiety, shallow chest breathing, and unconscious involuntary clenching of the bulbocavernosus muscle during thrusting) accelerates the spinal cord straight into Phase 1 Emission before the brain even realizes arousal has climbed past Level 7!
