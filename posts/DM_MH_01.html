Premature ejaculation: reducing pressure and finding support

Premature ejaculation is not judged by one encounter or a stopwatch alone.

PV
Pulse & Vitality • Health education
Primary reference: NHS — Ejaculation problems ↗ • Mapped Screen: Men's Health Screen • Biometric: Premature ejaculation: reducing pressure and finding support (educational topic; not a measured biomarker)
💡 Key Actionable Takeaway

Discuss pacing, pausing and comfortable activities with your partner. Recurrent, distressing difficulties can be addressed with a clinician or sex therapist who can explain treatment options.

Understanding the topic

Premature ejaculation is not judged by one encounter or a stopwatch alone. Perceived control, distress and context matter. Stress, sexual experiences and some health conditions can contribute. 1 2 3

What you can do

Discuss pacing, pausing and comfortable activities with your partner. Recurrent, distressing difficulties can be addressed with a clinician or sex therapist who can explain treatment options.

What the evidence cannot tell you

There is insufficient basis to promise that one Kegel contraction will lock the ejaculation reflex. Avoid forced tightening, breath-holding or straining as a required technique.

When to seek advice

New sudden difficulties, pain or urinary symptoms warrant assessment before relying on technique practice.

Sources and what they support

  1. NHS — Ejaculation problems — Public health guidance. Supports: Ejaculatory difficulties and treatment options.

  2. Royal Berkshire NHS (2024) — Pelvic floor strengthening and relaxation exercises for men — Physiotherapy patient guidance. Supports: Strengthening and relaxation; not a prescription to tighten for everyone.

  3. Devon Sexual Health (NHS) — Relationships and intimacy — Sexual health service guidance. Supports: Consent, intimacy and masturbation.

Content and sources updated: 2026-10-26. Educational content; clinical sign-off has not been recorded.
